Your first steps

  1. Start in Scratch: drag blocks to make a cat move, then add a score.
  2. Draw your own hero in Krita or MagicaVoxel.
  3. Make sound effects with one click in jsfxr.
  4. Share with family first — then try Roblox Studio or GDevelop.

A note for parents & teachers

  • Accounts: many tools need no account (Scratch to start, Bitsy, Twine, Krita, jsfxr). Scratch asks for a parent's email for under-13s; Roblox, Nintendo and Microsoft offer parent-managed accounts.
  • AI chatbots aren't for young kids: ChatGPT and Grok require 13+ with parental permission; Claude and Cursor require 18+. For under-13s, an adult should drive any AI help.
  • Sharing & chat: turn on parental controls for online sharing and chat (Roblox, Super Mario Maker 2), and talk about not sharing personal info.
  • Money: most picks here are free. Publishing on stores (itch.io, Steam, app stores) requires an adult or guardian consent.
  • Sit together: the first session goes best side by side — let the kid drive the mouse.

Age info reflects official terms and ratings as of 2026-10-13; always check the official site.
